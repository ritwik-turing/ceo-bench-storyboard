# CEO Bench demo storyboard

Internal production plan · September 23, 2026

**82 minutes, plus questions. 5 acts. 12 scenes. A full 60-minute workshop.**

The audience moves from understanding a company to doing professional work, defining a grading standard, and inspecting a model attempt against that standard.

## Operating assumptions

- L1 Director’s Room means the presenter’s orchestration layer in this storyboard. L3 and L2 refer to task levels, not screens. The L1 interpretation is a working assumption.
- One laptop and a big screen: journalists work on paper in parallel, then rotate through capture on the same laptop. The facilitator manages the audience view.
- Prepare 4 packets, each containing its prompt, 2–4 approved public source files, a blank deliverable sheet, and a blank rubric sheet. Expert reference answers and benchmark rubrics appear on screen only.
- The 60-minute workshop includes orientation, 22 minutes of task work, 16 minutes of rubric work, and 16 minutes for capture. Capture starts early for participants who finish first. Validate the timing in a dry run.
- The 5-minute grading segment examines 2 contrasting criteria in depth. It does not promise to grade all 4 rubrics completely.
- Full professional tasks use 10–60 weighted criteria; the current miniature supports 10–20. All weights total 100. A production expert reference answer must score 100% against its own rubric before shipping.

## Scene-by-scene script

### Scene 01 · Meet Cirrus Sleep

**00:00–02:00 · 2 minutes · Act 1: The company**

**Purpose:** Establish the company and the work to be evaluated.

**On screen:** Meet Cirrus Sleep

**Presenter narration**

> Cirrus Sleep is a fictional company built to evaluate artificial intelligence through professional work. Its dataset contains 1,100+ files covering a complete fiscal year. You’ll see how experts turn those records into tasks and define what a correct deliverable must do.

**Director action:** Open the company introduction. Keep the company profile and record count visible. Introduce Simulated Virtual Company (SVC) once, then use “the company” in the rest of the walkthrough.

**Journalist action:** Read the company profile. Consider what evidence you would need before accepting a financial or operational conclusion.

**Evidence and disclosure:** The 1,100+ count comes from the supplied demo outline. Attach the approved corpus manifest to the production asset list. Do not state that fictional origin guarantees permanent protection from training-data contamination.

**Transition:** “Let’s open the records behind that work.”

**Cue to continue:** Advance when the audience understands that the files are the working environment.

**Asset needed:** Company profile and approved corpus manifest.

**Build status:** Profile and count require production source mapping.

### Scene 02 · Explore a complete fiscal year

**02:00–05:00 · 3 minutes · Act 1: The company**

**Purpose:** Make the company tangible through its records.

**On screen:** Explore a complete fiscal year

**Presenter narration**

> You can follow the company through ledgers, statements, contracts, and decisions. A professional task may require you to connect several records and decide what each one establishes. Every conclusion you submit must be supported by the files provided.

**Director action:** Invite a journalist to choose a department. Open a public document, retain its filename and date, then open a related record. Do not expose the exercise’s answer or planted issue during this introduction.

**Journalist action:** Choose a folder and inspect 2 related records. Identify how the records might be used together.

**Evidence and disclosure:** Use only approved, already-public task records for full previews. The local prototype contains illustrative excerpts; retain its rehearsal label until the files are replaced.

**Transition:** “Now the board asks the team to prepare for an external audit.”

**Cue to continue:** Return to the company view after 2 documents, then advance.

**Asset needed:** 2 approved public documents with filenames, dates, and provenance.

**Build status:** Replace local rehearsal excerpts before a source-backed press demo.

### Scene 03 · Prepare for the first external audit

**05:00–07:00 · 2 minutes · Act 2: The breakdown**

**Purpose:** Establish the executive engagement and its professional scale.

**On screen:** Prepare for the first external audit

**Presenter narration**

> A practitioner starts with an executive engagement, written in the voice of the person responsible for the work. Here, the controller prepares the company for its first external audit, representing roughly 100 hours of professional effort. The answer has to be derivable from the files shipped with the task.

**Director action:** Reveal the role, objective, deliverable, and evidence boundary in that order. Label the source prompt “Abridged” if the on-screen version is shortened. Explain that level 3 (L3) is the executive task level, distinct from the Director’s Room interface.

**Journalist action:** Read the mandate and name a department whose work would be relevant.

**Evidence and disclosure:** Use the approved public engagement wording. “Roughly 100 hours” is the supplied task-design estimate, not a measured model runtime.

**Transition:** “The same company events support 10 independent professional tasks.”

**Cue to continue:** Hold the complete mandate for a readable beat before the task reveal.

**Asset needed:** Approved executive brief with source identifier.

**Build status:** Narrative defined; exact public prompt needs mapping.

### Scene 04 · Explore 10 independent tasks

**07:00–10:00 · 3 minutes · Act 2: The breakdown**

**Purpose:** Explain derivative tasks and assign the 4 seats.

**On screen:** Explore 10 independent tasks

**Presenter narration**

> Each level 2 (L2) task is a complete 10–20 hour assignment with its own prompt, expert reference answer, and rubric. These derivative tasks share the company’s story, and each is graded independently. Today, 4 workstreams become smaller journalist exercises.

**Director action:** Reveal 10 equal cards using a short stagger. Use a shared family connection, with no sequence arrows between tasks. Highlight cash, governance, audit evidence, and revenue. Add each journalist’s name to their assigned seat.

**Journalist action:** Locate the named seat and take the corresponding packet.

**Evidence and disclosure:** A derivative task must be independently answerable and gradeable. The reduced exercise does not reproduce the duration or complexity of the full professional assignment.

**Transition:** “You’ll complete the work first, then define how someone else’s answer should be assessed.”

**Cue to continue:** Advance once every journalist has a seat and its packet.

**Asset needed:** 10-task map, 4 prompts, seat assignments, and paper packets.

**Build status:** Current prototype supports task reveal and named seats.

### Scene 05 · Take your seat

**10:00–16:00 · 6 minutes · Act 3: Creating a task**

**Purpose:** Give every participant a clear assignment and working method.

**On screen:** Take your seat

**Presenter narration**

> Your packet contains 2–4 source files and a short professional assignment. Work from the evidence, record your reasoning, and make any uncertainty visible. You’ll complete an expert reference answer, also called a golden, and then write the criteria used to grade another attempt.

**Director action:** Explain the deliverable format and source references. Show the empty workstation without a prefilled answer. Explain the single-laptop rotation: parallel paper work, then sequential capture in the browser. Start the 60-minute workshop clock.

**Journalist action:** Read the prompt and packet. Check that the seat label and file references match. Ask clarifying questions about the assignment, not its solution.

**Evidence and disclosure:** Give participants the prompt and approved source files only. Do not distribute expert reference answers or benchmark rubrics. Supply blank answer and rubric worksheets.

**Transition:** “Start with the deliverable. Show what the records support.”

**Cue to continue:** Begin work when all 4 seats can state the output they owe.

**Asset needed:** 4 labeled packets, blank worksheets, pens, and permitted calculator.

**Build status:** Workstation exists; final paper packet assembly remains.

### Scene 06 · Build your answer from the evidence

**16:00–38:00 · 22 minutes · Act 3: Creating a task**

**Purpose:** Let the journalist experience the judgment required by the task.

**On screen:** Build your answer from the evidence

**Presenter narration**

> Complete the deliverable yourself and connect each conclusion to its source. If the records disagree or a document is missing, state what you can establish and what still needs investigation. Avoid filling an evidence gap with an assumption presented as fact.

**Director action:** Keep the big screen quiet, showing the time and work instructions. Support navigation and arithmetic questions without giving away findings. At the midpoint, remind participants to cite filenames or packet references.

**Journalist action:** Produce a concise memo or worksheet. Identify relevant facts, document status, conflicting evidence, and a justified next action.

**Evidence and disclosure:** Findings must come from each seat’s actual packet. Do not promise that every journalist finds the intended issue. Preserve the participant’s answer for subsequent review.

**Transition:** “Now write down what another answer must do to meet your standard.”

**Cue to continue:** Move to rubric authoring when each participant has a draft deliverable.

**Asset needed:** Source packets and participant-authored deliverables.

**Build status:** Paper work fits the one-laptop setup; prototype also supports typed memos.

### Scene 07 · Define what a correct answer requires

**38:00–54:00 · 16 minutes · Act 3: Creating a task**

**Purpose:** Make expert judgment explicit and evidence-based.

**On screen:** Define what a correct answer requires

**Presenter narration**

> Think about how a teacher grades an essay: each criterion describes something the answer must do, with points for its importance. A full professional task has 10–60 weighted criteria totaling 100 points; this exercise uses 10–20. A must-pass miss fails the attempt, and the expert’s own reference answer must earn 100% against its rubric before a real task ships.

**Director action:** Show 1 neutral example of a criterion, its weight, its evidence reference, and its pass condition. Do not reveal a seat-specific expected answer. Explain that criteria must assess the requested work and that must-pass gates need a professional-integrity rationale.

**Journalist action:** Write 10–20 testable criteria, assign positive weights totaling 100, and mark justified must-pass gates. Check the draft reference answer against every criterion and revise unsupported or ambiguous wording.

**Evidence and disclosure:** The 100-point structure is a format check. Correctness requires source review. Independent expert review of a production task cannot be replaced by the workshop’s brief check.

**Transition:** “Let’s capture your deliverable and put the grading criteria into review.”

**Cue to continue:** Capture early finishers during this period to reduce the laptop queue.

**Asset needed:** Blank 10-row rubric sheet, weight total, and a neutral criterion example.

**Build status:** Prototype enforces criterion count, written text, positive weights, and a 100-point total.

### Scene 08 · Submit your work for review

**54:00–70:00 · 16 minutes · Act 3: Creating a task**

**Purpose:** Show the handoff from authoring to review.

**On screen:** Submit your work for review

**Presenter narration**

> Each seat now submits its deliverable and rubric into review. The app checks that the required fields are present and the points total 100; an expert still checks the evidence and the answer. Your work becomes visible on the presentation screen when you submit it.

**Director action:** Rotate seats through the laptop, allowing about 4 minutes each and starting earlier when possible. Have a facilitator capture the full deliverable, enter the rubric, and ask the journalist to confirm the text and weights before submission. Open 1 submission on screen and demonstrate a source check.

**Journalist action:** Verify the captured deliverable and rubric, then choose “Send to review.” Explain 1 criterion to the group. Keep paper materials with the facilitator under the event handling plan.

**Evidence and disclosure:** File or photo capture is a production requirement for fast paper handoff. The current prototype accepts typed memos only. For a prototype dry run, use short typed summaries and label sample submissions as rehearsal examples.

**Transition:** “You’ve defined acceptable work. Now compare that standard with model performance.”

**Cue to continue:** Advance after the intended submissions are visible; do not invent a receipt for a missing submission.

**Asset needed:** Captured deliverables, rubric entries, and an agreed paper-handling procedure.

**Build status:** Live same-browser inbox works. File/photo upload and cross-device transport are not built.

### Scene 09 · Read the published benchmark result

**70:00–73:00 · 3 minutes · Act 4: The benchmark**

**Purpose:** Explain the measured result and what the metric means.

**On screen:** Read the published benchmark result

**Presenter narration**

> In this published snapshot, the highest all-core pass rate among the evaluated model and harness combinations is 20.3%. That measures trials clearing every core criterion, not the average share of points earned. Weighted rubric reward is reported separately.

**Director action:** Reveal the verified all-core figures with a true 0–100% scale. Keep the source, snapshot date, and scope visible. Say “among the evaluated combinations,” rather than implying coverage of every current model.

**Journalist action:** Read the metric definition and distinguish a full task pass from partial credit.

**Evidence and disclosure:** CEO Bench About page: 30 tasks, 1,078 trials, and 6 model/harness combinations. Snapshot dated September 17, 2026; checked September 23, 2026. Refresh immediately before the event. The public leaderboard itself ranks weighted reward, so label this view “All-core pass rate.”

**Transition:** “Let’s inspect an attempt through the criteria you just wrote.”

**Cue to continue:** Proceed once the distinction between all-core pass rate and weighted reward is clear.

**Asset needed:** Dated public result snapshot and source link.

**Build status:** Published figures verified; refresh required for the event date.

### Scene 10 · Apply your rubric to an attempt

**73:00–78:00 · 5 minutes · Act 4: The benchmark**

**Purpose:** Make a grading judgment visible, without predetermining its outcome.

**On screen:** Apply your rubric to an attempt

**Presenter narration**

> We’ll review a recorded model attempt against criteria you’ve written today. For each criterion, we’ll read the attempt and source evidence before assigning a judgment. A criterion may recognize correct work, reveal a failure, or need clarification before it can be scored.

**Director action:** Choose 2 contrasting criteria across the submitted rubrics, as time allows. Show the criterion, attempt excerpt, source evidence, and reviewer judgment together. Reveal 1 judgment at a time. Provide an unscored or needs-clarification path and exclude those items from any complete-score claim.

**Journalist action:** Explain the selected criterion, inspect the evidence, and agree or challenge the reviewer’s judgment.

**Evidence and disclosure:** Production mode requires a genuine recorded attempt with model, harness, task version, and run date. Label the action “Manual evidence review.” Prototype mode uses “Scripted rehearsal attempt”; say explicitly that no model was run. Do not preassign failure to arbitrary journalist criteria.

**Transition:** “The same specific feedback can help train and evaluate models.”

**Cue to continue:** Show the separate outcomes: points earned, must-pass status, and completeness of review.

**Asset needed:** A real recorded attempt, source references, and per-criterion review notes.

**Build status:** Prototype has fixture annotations and manual pass/fail. Genuine attempt integration and a needs-clarification state remain.

### Scene 11 · Use task feedback to improve models

**78:00–80:00 · 2 minutes · Act 5: Training**

**Purpose:** Connect evaluation to possible training workflows.

**On screen:** Use task feedback to improve models

**Presenter narration**

> For frontier labs, rubric results can provide a reward signal during reinforcement learning. Enterprises can fine-tune on training examples, then evaluate on separate, held-out tasks. Passing a simulation can be one deployment gate, alongside the controls needed for the real environment.

**Director action:** Show separate paths for lab training and enterprise adaptation. Keep the held-out evaluation set visually distinct from training data. Reveal the deployment gate only after the evaluation step.

**Journalist action:** Identify a criterion that offers a useful learning signal and explain why it should remain meaningful on unseen work.

**Evidence and disclosure:** These are potential workflows, not measured improvements demonstrated by this prototype. Do not animate an invented lift in performance or imply that evaluation alone guarantees deployment readiness.

**Transition:** “Next, test whether that capability carries into new company events.”

**Cue to continue:** Advance after the separation between training and held-out evaluation is understood.

**Asset needed:** Training/evaluation diagram and approved terminology.

**Build status:** Conceptual training paths are already represented in the prototype.

### Scene 12 · Test what carries into year 2

**80:00–82:00 · 2 minutes · Act 5: Training**

**Purpose:** Close with the evidence chain and a bounded next step.

**On screen:** Test what carries into year 2

**Presenter narration**

> A proposed year 2 scenario introduces a Series B and the investor’s perspective on the company. It creates new work for a separate evaluation. Today, you’ve followed the chain from company records to a professional deliverable, a rubric, and an evidence-based judgment.

**Director action:** Show the proposed year 2 scenario without a release date. Close on the 4 seat titles and the chain from records to evaluation. Invite a brief response before opening general questions.

**Journalist action:** Name 1 criterion they would retain and 1 question they would investigate next.

**Evidence and disclosure:** Keep “Proposed extension” visible unless launch status is confirmed. Do not show unsupplied year 2 records or unannounced benchmark results.

**Transition:** “What would you need to verify before relying on this work?”

**Cue to continue:** End the formal 82-minute sequence; questions follow outside this timing.

**Asset needed:** Approved year 2 wording and the session’s actual submission status.

**Build status:** Teaser is proposed; no new release claim is made.

## Brand application

This artifact follows the internal Turing visual system. It is a storyboard and production specification; it does not restyle the existing demo app.

| Element | Application |
| --- | --- |
| Logo | Approved full black Turing SVG, unmodified, at least 120px wide on screen, with clear surrounding space. |
| Typography | Locally bundled Poppins regular and semibold throughout the internal storyboard. |
| Color | Black, white, and gray foundations. Dark blue #003F9B for small text and actions; #317EFF for selected emphasis; #EEFBFF for supporting panels. |
| Case and punctuation | Sentence case, numerals, Oxford commas, expanded technical terms, and no em dashes. |
| Voice | Direct, credible, and specific. Evidence precedes judgment. Avoid promises about contamination, guaranteed model failure, or unmeasured training gains. |
| Motion | Short, presenter-controlled reveals. Keep the full evidence and labels readable after each reveal. No decorative motion during task work. |
| Disclosure | Published results, genuine recorded attempts, and authored rehearsal content are labeled separately. |

**Typography decision:** The July documents restrict APK Galeria to external marketing headings and prohibit mixing it with Poppins without specifying an external body font. Poppins throughout is the unambiguous internal-document choice. External marketing typography still needs that pairing resolved. The linked external-design file was inaccessible through the connected Drive account.

**Source precedence:** The content playbook is dated July 30, 2026, and the logo guide is updated July 2026. The broad guide’s filename says 2026, while its cover says 2025. Where writing rules conflict, this storyboard follows the later playbook; this is an editorial interpretation.
